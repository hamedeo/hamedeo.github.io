Years ago, I worked at Mobarakeh Steel Company near Isfahan.

What stayed with me was not the scale of the machinery. It was the people who kept it running: operators, technicians, engineers, drivers, contractors and thousands of families whose lives were tied to that place.

![Hamed Abdollahi wearing blue protective workwear and a hard hat inside the control room at Mobarakeh Steel Company](./Pics/MobarakehSteelFactory-HamedAbdollahi.jpg "class:article-image article-image--half")

> **A factory is never just a factory.**

It is wages, food, rent, education and security. It connects workers to suppliers, transport companies, workshops and entire communities. When it is attacked, the damage does not stop at the factory gates. It moves through society, while those with the least power carry the greatest cost.

Iranian people have already paid heavily for demanding dignity, freedom and basic rights. Their suffering under domestic repression must not be ignored.

> **But destroying the country’s workplaces and infrastructure does not free them.**

Those who hold power usually protect themselves. Workers lose income. Families face uncertainty. Communities become weaker. The people already carrying the burden are asked to carry more.

No government has the right to silence its people. No foreign power has the right to present their suffering as the price of liberation.

A steel plant is built by generations of labour, knowledge and sacrifice. Its true owners are not only those whose names appear on official documents. It also belongs, in a deeper sense, to everyone whose work gave it value and everyone whose future depends on it.

There is another lesson here.

It is easy to express concern for suffering far away. It is harder to stand beside someone facing injustice nearby: a colleague being exploited, a worker being dismissed unfairly, a neighbour unable to cope, or a person being silenced because they have less power.

Distant solidarity matters. But when it replaces responsibility within our reach, it becomes performance.

Real help moves from person to person. It is built through courage, attention and mutual responsibility.

Iran will not be rebuilt by rulers, missiles or slogans. It will be rebuilt by people who understand that their lives are connected.

> **In the end, we have each other.**

That is where resistance begins.

That is where rebuilding begins.
